Program overview
	•	The University of Arizona B.S. in Software Engineering (SFWE) prepares students to design, build and maintain complex software systems that support an increasingly connected world. ￼
	•	Students learn the full software development lifecycle (SDLC) – from requirements and architecture through implementation, assurance, DevOps and maintenance. ￼
	•	The program is administered by the Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering (ECE) and the College of Engineering, and is available on:
	•	Main Campus (Tucson)
	•	Arizona Online
	•	Yuma and Chandler locations (via Near You Network)  ￼

Academic focus areas

Students build depth across topics that span the entire SDLC and large-scale product development, including: ￼
	•	Software requirements analysis and testing
	•	Software architecture and design
	•	Software assurance and security
	•	DevOps, CI/CD and agile methods
	•	Project management and cost estimation
	•	Digital logic, computer organization and embedded systems

Electives allow students to specialize in areas such as:
	•	Web and mobile applications
	•	Embedded and cyber-physical systems
	•	Data analytics, machine learning and AI
	•	Cloud, networks and cybersecurity  ￼

Research & experiential learning

Students can get involved in software-related research in areas such as: ￼
	•	Autonomous systems and robotics
	•	Cloud/distributed computing and computer architecture
	•	Data analytics, informatics and machine learning
	•	Embedded systems and wireless networking / security

Hands-on opportunities include:
	•	Design projects every semester, culminating in an interdisciplinary capstone sequence (ENGR 498A/B). ￼
	•	Internships with long-term industry partners.
	•	Student organizations and national competitions (IEEE, IISE/INCOSE, Software Engineering Wildcats, and other engineering clubs that support diversity and inclusion). ￼

Career outcomes

Graduates are prepared for roles such as:
	•	Software or applications engineer / developer
	•	Software architect
	•	DevOps / cloud engineer
	•	Systems engineer
	•	Project manager or technical lead

Employers span both industry and government (examples in the source doc include large tech companies, defense contractors, health tech companies, financial institutions, and national labs). ￼

BS curriculum snapshot (Main Campus, Catalog Year 2025–26)

Note: This is a simplified, human-readable summary of the official 4-year plan. Students must always check the current General Catalog and meet with an advisor for final requirements. ￼

Year 1
Fall (approx. 14–16 units)  ￼
	•	Calculus I (MATH 122A/B or 125)
	•	Intro chemistry with lab (CHEM 151 or 161/163) – also counts as Natural Science Gen Ed
	•	First-Year Composition (ENGL 101/107/109H)
	•	Introduction to Engineering (ENGR 102A/B or ENGR 102)
	•	UNIV 101 – General Education Experience

Spring (approx. 17 units)  ￼
	•	Calculus II (MATH 129)
	•	SFWE 101 – Introduction to Software Engineering
	•	ECE 101 – Programming I
	•	Second composition course (ENGL 102 or 108)
	•	Second science course (CHEM 152 or 162/164, or MSE 110, or MCB 181R/L)

Year 2
Fall (approx. 16 units)  ￼
	•	Discrete Math for CS (MATH 243)
	•	Physics I: Mechanics (PHYS 141 or 161H) – can also fulfill Natural Science Gen Ed
	•	ECE 201 – Programming II
	•	Gen Ed: Exploring Perspectives (Artist)
	•	Gen Ed: Exploring Perspectives (Humanist)

Spring (approx. 15 units)  ￼
	•	SIE 277 – Object-Oriented Modeling & Design
	•	ECE 274A – Digital Logic
	•	SFWE 201 – Software Engineering Sophomore Colloquium
	•	Physics II: Electricity & Magnetism (PHYS 241 or 261H)
	•	Math / Statistics elective (advisor-approved)

Advanced Standing is required before taking 300- and 400-level engineering courses. ￼

Year 3
Fall (approx. 13 units)  ￼
	•	SIE 305 – Engineering Probability & Statistics
	•	SFWE 301 – Software Requirements Analysis & Test
	•	SFWE 303 – Data Persistence
	•	ECE 369A – Fundamentals of Computer Organization

Spring (approx. 16 units)  ￼
	•	SFWE 302 or SFWE 405 – Software Architecture & Design
	•	SFWE 401 – Software Assurance & Security
	•	ECE 311 – Engineering Ethics and Contemporary Issues
	•	CSC 355 or CSC 345 – discrete structures / algorithms
	•	Technical elective (advisor-approved)
	•	Gen Ed: Building Connections

Year 4
Fall (approx. 15 units)  ￼
	•	SFWE 403 – Software Project Management
	•	ENGR 498A – Interdisciplinary Capstone I
	•	Gen Ed: Exploring Perspectives (Social Scientist)
	•	Technical elective
	•	Gen Ed: Building Connections

Spring (approx. 14–16 units)  ￼
	•	SFWE 402 – DevOps
	•	Technical electives (1–3 + 3 units)
	•	ENGR 498B – Interdisciplinary Capstone II
	•	Gen Ed: Building Connections
	•	UNIV 301 – General Education Portfolio

Students work with their advisor to select technical electives and Building Connections courses; some technical electives may also fulfill Gen Ed requirements. ￼
