Software Engineering Technical Elective Tracks (Guide)

This guide explains the optional technical elective tracks available to BS Software Engineering students. It is meant as a planning tool only. Course offerings and availability by campus or modality can change; students must always confirm details with the official catalog and their academic advisor.  ￼

Tracks are not printed on the transcript, but they help you:
	•	Build a coherent skill set in a specific area
	•	Choose 4 or more technical electives that work together
	•	Communicate a focus area to employers or graduate programs

The main suggested tracks are:
	1.	AI / Machine Learning
	2.	Algorithms & Computational Methods
	3.	Cloud Computing
	4.	Cybersecurity
	5.	Data Science / Analytics
	6.	Embedded & Systems

⸻

1. AI / Machine Learning Track

Focus: Intelligent systems, modeling, and decision-making.
This track prepares you for roles in machine learning, analytics, data-driven products, and intelligent systems.

Representative courses:
	•	SIE 433 – Fundamentals of Data Science for Engineers (3)
	•	Campus: Main, Yuma  ￼
	•	SIE 431 – Simulation Modeling & Analysis (3)
	•	Campus: Main  ￼
	•	SIE 430 – Engineering Statistics (3)
	•	Campus: Main, Online  ￼
	•	GAME 426 – Game Artificial Intelligence (3)
	•	Campus: Main, Yuma  ￼
	•	ECE 479 – Artificial Intelligence (3)
	•	Campus: Main  ￼
	•	CSC 471 (or APCV/CSC(V) 471) – Artificial Intelligence (3)
	•	Campus: Online, Yuma (availability varies)  ￼
	•	ECE 425 – Intro to Deep Learning: An Engineering Perspective (3)
	•	Campus: Main  ￼
	•	CSC 477 – Intro to Computer Vision (3)
	•	Campus: Main  ￼

Typical outcomes: ML engineer, data scientist (entry-level), simulation engineer, AI engineer, technical developer on analytics/ML teams.

⸻

2. Algorithms & Computational Methods Track

Focus: Advanced algorithms, numerical methods, and analytical modeling.
Emphasizes rigorous problem-solving and performance-oriented computation.

Representative courses:
	•	SIE 431 – Simulation Modeling & Analysis (3)
	•	Campus: Main, Yuma  ￼
	•	ECE 429 – Digital Signal Processing (3)
	•	Campus: Main, Online  ￼
	•	ECE 330B – Computational Techniques (3)
	•	Campus: Main  ￼

Typical outcomes: Software engineer with strong algorithms background, roles in optimization, signal processing, simulation and modeling-heavy fields.

⸻

3. Cloud Computing Track

Focus: Building and operating software in modern cloud environments.
This pairs very naturally with DevOps and large-scale distributed systems.

Representative courses:
	•	SFWE 410 – Cloud Native (temporarily SIE 496) (3)
	•	Campus: Main, Online, Yuma  ￼
	•	SFWE 409 – Principles of Cloud Computing (3)
	•	Campus: Main, Online, Yuma  ￼

Typical outcomes: Cloud / DevOps engineer, backend or platform engineer, roles that involve microservices, containers, and cloud-native architectures.

⸻

	•	Campus: Main, Yuma  ￼

	•	Campus: Main  ￼
	•	CYBV 480 – Cyber Warfare (3) – Online  ￼
	•	CYBV 474 – Advanced Analytics for Security Operations (3) – Online  ￼
	•	CYBV 473 – Violent Python (3) – Online  ￼

Typical outcomes: Security engineer, security-focused software engineer, SOC analyst (with more SOC/CYBV electives), or systems engineer for critical infrastructure.

⸻

5. Data Science / Analytics Track

Focus: Data engineering, data analysis, visualization, and analytics tooling.
Ideal if you want your SFWE core + strong data skills for data platforms, analytics-heavy apps, or ML pipelines.

Representative courses (examples from the tech elective table and related listings):
	•	SIE 433 – Fundamentals of Data Science for Engineers (3) – also listed under AI/ML
	•	SIE 431 – Simulation Modeling & Analysis (3) – modeling & analytics focus  ￼
	•	SIE 430 – Engineering Statistics (3)  ￼
	•	ISTA 322 – Data Engineering (3)  ￼
	•	ISTA 321 – Data Mining and Discovery (3)  ￼
	•	ISTA 320 – Applied Data Visualization (3)  ￼
	•	ISTA 431 – Data Warehousing and Analytics in the Cloud (3)  ￼

(Availability and campus offerings vary; see catalog and advisor.)

Typical outcomes: Software engineer with a data emphasis, data engineer, analytics platform developer, or a bridge role between software engineering and data science.

⸻

6. Embedded & Systems Track

Focus: Software that interacts tightly with hardware, real-time systems, and low-level control.
Good for students interested in robotics, IoT, automotive, and industrial systems.

Representative courses (examples):
	•	SIE 370 – Embedded Computer Systems (4)  ￼
	•	SFWE 411 – Software for Industrial Control Systems (3)  ￼
	•	ECE 476/576, 576A/B and related embedded/CBSE offerings (listed elsewhere in graduate and advanced course info; check with advisor for undergrad availability).  ￼

Typical outcomes: Embedded software engineer, robotics software engineer, systems engineer, or roles in hardware–software co-design.

⸻

How to Use These Tracks
	1.	Pick 1–2 tracks that align with your interests (for example, Cloud + Cyber, or AI/ML + Data).
	2.	For each track, select at least 3–4 courses as your technical electives, making sure you meet all prerequisites.
	3.	Confirm:
	•	Course offerings by campus (Main, Online, Yuma, Chandler)
	•	Prerequisites (e.g., SIE 305, ECE 175, CSC 355/345, etc.)
	•	Whether the course can count as a tech elective in your specific catalog year. ￼
	4.	Discuss your plan with your academic advisor to verify it satisfies degree requirements and fits your 4-year plan.
